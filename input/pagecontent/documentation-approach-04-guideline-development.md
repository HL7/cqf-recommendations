Develop a clinically justified recommendation before deciding how software will deliver it. This section explains the handoff from guideline development to computable content; it does not replace a complete guideline-development method.

### Guideline Development Group

Assign responsibility for evidence appraisal and recommendation approval. Include the clinical disciplines and patients/caregivers affected by the guidance, methodological expertise, and relevant implementation, terminology, informatics, communication, and evaluation roles. One person may cover several responsibilities. Clinical interpretation remains a reviewed decision rather than an implicit choice by a tool or agent.

The [integrated process](https://doi.org/10.1097/JMQ.0000000000000137) describes co-development by these disciplines. It is a process framework; faster adoption and better outcomes remain objectives to evaluate.

### Scoping

Record the population, intervention, comparator, outcomes, timing, and setting (PICOTS), the intended users, exclusions, source/version, and the decision the guideline will support. Add feasibility questions early: which required facts are available, who can act, and what local systems can support the intended intervention?

### Guideline Development Frameworks

Use the [framework comparison](documentation-approach-04-02-guideline-development-frameworks.html) to choose the evidence and recommendation-development methods. Keep the method, assumptions, and responsible reviewers visible in the resulting content.

### Development of Computable Clinical Guideline Artifacts

Hand off a reviewable set of work products rather than a final PDF alone:

| Work product | What the next team needs |
| --- | --- |
| Source and scope | Stable recommendation identity, publication/version, population and settings |
| Evidence and rationale | Citations, certainty, strength, direction, tradeoffs, and acknowledged gaps |
| Explicit decisions | Applicability, exclusions, timing, exceptions, intended action and responsible actor |
| Concepts and examples | Definitions, candidate data sources, missing-data behavior, representative cases |
| Implementation and evaluation plan | Delivery constraints, local review owners, measures of use, effect, and unintended consequences |
