# Publication evidence for the narrative refresh

Research date and cutoff: **2026-10-28**. This is an editorial narrative review, not a systematic review or a clinical recommendation. It supports a rewrite for human and agent implementors without changing conformance artifacts, publisher configuration, or templates.

## Search approach and limits

The search deliberately covered both standards-based implementation and CDS generally. Sources were located through web searches targeting PubMed/PMC, publisher full text, and WHO, CDC, AHRQ, and ASTP/ONC publications. PubMed records were used to check citation metadata and abstracts; PMC, publisher, and official full text were read where accessible. Europe PMC's full-text XML service provided a supplementary access path for several PMC papers. Intermittent NCBI bot checks and publisher errors meant that some checks relied on indexed primary-source excerpts or abstracts, as marked below.

Representative search families actually used:

- `FHIR CQL CDS Hooks implementation clinical decision support multisite study PubMed`; `FHIR CQL clinical decision support portability testing versioning terminology implementation study 2024 2025`; and the same standards terms with `2026`.
- `CDS Connect lessons learned implementation AHRQ final report standards CQL`.
- `WHO SMART guidelines Experiences aligning classification terminology standards 2023`; integrated guideline development and evaluation framework titles; WHO digital adaptation kits; GUIDE implementation research.
- `clinical decision support alert fatigue overrides systematic review`; `clinical decision support malfunctions`; generic CDS effectiveness, care-process and patient-outcome reviews; nearly hard-stop alert trial; electronic VTE prevention alerts.
- Exact-title searches and citation-following to verify authors, dates, identifiers, methods, and limitations.

Selection was purposive: directly relevant foundational work, empirical implementation studies, general CDS outcome/safety syntheses, and newer publications with useful implementor lessons. No formal screening denominator, duplicate-independent review, exhaustive database export, language-complete search, or risk-of-bias assessment was performed. This collection is therefore unsuitable for claims that it contains all relevant studies or establishes an overall effect of standards-based CDS. It favors English-language, publicly accessible material, with substantial US/academic-center representation. Several reviews contain overlapping primary studies; do not combine their counts or treat them as independent replications.

Thirty sources are retained: 19 standards/computable-guideline sources and 11 general CDS or operational-safety sources. The latest selected empirical standards implementation is from July 2026. A publication date does not date the underlying experiment: for example, the 2026 STI paper describes a 2024 pilot. Standards features discussed in older papers are historical observations, not a statement of current specification or vendor support.

## How to use the evidence

- **Measured clinical effect:** a patient outcome was measured in a comparative study. Context and design still limit generalizability.
- **Measured behavior/use/accuracy:** an implementation, process, utilization, or technical result was measured; this does not establish patient benefit.
- **Implementation experience:** direct project experience, qualitative evaluation, or case study; useful for feasibility and failure modes, not a causal effectiveness estimate.
- **Framework, specification, or protocol:** describes a method, intended design, or planned evaluation. Benefits are objectives unless evaluated separately.
- **Scholarly synthesis:** summarizes other studies; the strength and applicability depend on its scope, search dates, study quality, and heterogeneity.

The implementor lessons in the final column are **editorial implications**, not new normative requirements or effects proven by the cited source. Proposed narrative locations describe topics; they do not authorize changing frozen resource definitions.

## General CDS evidence and operational safety

These sources support design and evaluation principles across CDS. They do **not** demonstrate that adopting FHIR, CQL, CDS Hooks, SMART App Launch, or this IG causes the effects observed.

## Synthesis for the rewrite

### 1. Open with the practical job and a calibrated claim

The guide's `index.md` currently mixes its purpose, a broad clinical-quality ecosystem, a large integrated process, and ambitious efficiency/benefit goals. A clearer opening can say that the guide supports expressing guideline intent as reusable computable knowledge and connecting that knowledge to local systems. Reuse is an engineering objective; local readiness and outcomes remain separate work. S03, S09, and S19 give direct implementation reasons for this qualification. Generic CDS can improve care, but G06 and G10 support qualified language about context and outcome type rather than blanket effectiveness claims.

Suggested claim pattern: **“Standards support sharing and integrating computable guidance. Each deployment still needs validation of clinical meaning, available data, supported workflow, and resulting effects.”** This is an editorial synthesis of the evidence, not a verbatim claim from a study.

### 2. Explain the layers as connected deliverables

S01 supplies the foundational rationale; S10–S13 and S18 show how interdisciplinary guideline work and software-neutral operational specifications fit. For each layer, state its input, output, accountable reviewers, and link to the same recommendation in other representations. A computable artifact may be executable in a suitable engine and still require local data, terminology, action, authorization, or UI integration. Avoid diagrams that imply a layer transition automatically proves fidelity or deployment readiness.

For agent implementors, a useful documentation contract would identify the source recommendation and version, target population, exact data meaning, decision expression, expected action, dependencies, test cases, unresolved ambiguities, and human reviewer. This is a proposed documentation aid, not a new artifact schema or normative profile.

### 3. Give the standards distinct responsibilities

S02 and S06 support a compact component map: FHIR for the resource/data and knowledge-artifact model; CQL for clinical expressions; CDS Hooks for invoking external support at supported workflow events; SMART App Launch for launching/authorizing apps with context. Confirm current details in the published specifications pinned by this repository. The literature is evidence about use, not the authority for present normative behavior.

Separate the term **WHO SMART Guidelines** from **SMART App Launch** on first use. The former is a guideline-digitalization programme; the latter is an application interoperability specification.

### 4. Reframe integration as a choice with explicit prerequisites

The current integration narrative offers ingestion, service, enrichment, app, and hybrid patterns and sometimes makes broad claims about the usual/preferred pattern. Literature here supports explaining tradeoffs and local constraints; it does not establish enrichment as a generally superior default. For every pattern show where logic runs, where patient data come from, what event triggers evaluation, how results reach a user or record, what local capability is required, and who maintains the integration. S03, S07, S09, and S19 are particularly useful anchors.

A SMART app may call a reasoning service; a CDS Hooks response may launch an app. Diagram these as compositions rather than mutually exclusive products. Persistence of derived clinical information also needs a stated owner and lifecycle; service invocation alone does not establish those decisions.

### 5. Make testing a sequence of different questions

S04, S15, S16, and S19 supply concrete, bounded test examples. G07 and G09 explain why successful predeployment tests do not end the work. A narrative can distinguish:

1. **Clinical meaning:** Do clinicians agree that the structured decision preserves recommendation intent and exceptions?
2. **Logic behavior:** Do positive, negative, boundary, missing-data, and temporal cases produce expected results?
3. **Data and terminology:** Do actual local encodings, units, statuses, and missingness satisfy the logic's assumptions?
4. **Execution compatibility:** Do the chosen versions of artifacts, libraries, terminology, model, and engine work together?
5. **Integration behavior:** Do trigger, authorization, data retrieval, result rendering, and actions work in the target environment?
6. **Workflow and operations:** Can intended people act at the intended time, and are failures detected after release?
7. **Effects:** What changes in care, patient outcomes, workload, equity, or unintended harm result?

This sequence is an editorial organization, not a validated universal test protocol. Preserve the distinction between a prototype, synthetic test, retrospective test, production deployment, and comparative clinical evaluation.

### 6. Treat terminology and versioning as ongoing dependencies

S13 demonstrates why a mapped code can be only an approximation of the narrative concept. G07 demonstrates operational failures after code/value changes and changes to shared components. Accordingly, document mapping qualifications, source/terminology/library versions, expected local representations, and which dependents must be rechecked after change. The precise canonical/version semantics belong to the repository's pinned CRMI/CQL/FHIR specifications; these papers do not define them.

### 7. Keep humans, patients, and work visible

S07 shows that a standard-supported recommendation can still encounter UI launch friction. S14 expands the workflow beyond a clinician at a visit: who reviews incoming patient data, what happens after hours, and who handles erroneous data matter. G01 supports timely actionable support; G04/G08 show why “accepted” and “overridden” need clinical context; G05 supports explicitly assessing adverse workflow effects. Agent-facing clarity should make these human decisions easier to find, not imply autonomous clinical authority.

### 8. Show evaluation and governance as loops

S11 separates the process of producing guidance, the quality of its products, and their outcomes. G07/G09/G11 support named maintenance and monitoring responsibilities after publication. A simple original lifecycle diagram can show recommendation development, computable specification, validation, local integration, monitoring/evaluation, and revision, with feedback to relevant stages. Avoid a single success checkmark that appears to certify all these outcomes.

## Uncertainties and editorial safeguards

- **No pooled standards-specific effectiveness claim.** This collection does not establish that FHIR/CQL/CDS Hooks/SMART interventions are more clinically effective, cheaper, or faster than alternatives. Some studies show feasibility, usage, accuracy, or bounded portability; these are different endpoints.
- **Version and capability drift.** Check the published standards and this repository's dependency versions before rewriting technical statements. Do not silently substitute a current CI build or a newer standard for the version this guide uses. Historical EHR limitations may have changed; local capability discovery still needs current verification.
- **Protocol versus results.** S17 is an implementation-research protocol; no outcome claim should be extracted from planned comparisons. The review did not establish whether a later GUIDE results publication exists.
- **Synthetic and retrospective tests.** S04/S15/S16 have useful test evidence with bounded datasets/use cases. None proves correctness for every patient, data representation, engine, or care setting.
- **Publication metadata.** S03 is indexed in the 2019 proceedings but electronically published in 2020; G07 appeared online in 2017 and in a 2018 issue. G11's release naming and PDF footer dates differ. Preserve these distinctions rather than assigning dates from search-engine crawl labels.
- **Rights and diagrams.** Public access is not figure-reuse permission. Several accessible articles use noncommercial/no-derivatives or conventional publisher terms; no license inventory or permission request has been completed. Create original diagrams from the guide's own concepts, cite intellectual sources, and review any proposed close adaptation or reproduced figure separately. No external figure has been downloaded or reused for this research note.
- **Normative boundaries.** The papers support explanatory narrative, examples, and evaluation principles. They do not override FHIR/CQL/CDS Hooks/SMART conformance rules, clinical policy, local authorization, or the frozen artifacts in this task.
- **Search limits.** AI/LLM CDS papers were encountered but not selected merely for recency. Their additional failure modes and evidence would warrant a separately scoped review if the narrative later makes substantive AI-specific clinical claims.
